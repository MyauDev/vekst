## 5. What I would do

**1. Answer one question first: is there a second developer?**
Everything else follows from it. If the answer is no, the plan must be rewritten
for one person before any new change starts.

**2. Escalate D-2 today, not at the next sync.**
It is ten days late and it blocks the whole of Track A. There is no substitute
for it, because the plan bans a Demo on invented data.

**3. Cut scope. These four changes are safe to move out of the Demo:**

| # | Change | Days | Why it is safe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.2 | `add-identity` | 1.5 | You create both users yourself for a Demo. |

That releases 7 planned days and leaves 24. It is not enough. At the measured
overrun 24 becomes ≈ 51 person-days.

**4. Therefore move the date as well.** The evidence supports:

- One developer, with the cut: ≈ 64 working days → early December.
- Two developers, with the cut: ≈ 32 working days → late October.

Pick the date from the headcount you actually have.

**5. Do not cut these**, whatever the pressure: tenancy and forced RLS, the
`entity` level, `int64` money, append-only classifications, balance
reconciliation, internal-transfer detection and drill-down. Retro-fitting any of
them costs more than the Demo.

**6. Give the provisioning change an owner and 2–3 days**, or state now that
the Demo runs on a laptop. Both are acceptable. Silence is not.

**7. Then propose 1.1 `add-tenancy-and-rls` and start.** It blocks every table
that follows it.

once — the 31 remaining days do not fit the 30 usable days of two developers,
and they are double the 15 of one. The date fails on the optimistic assumption
as well.
